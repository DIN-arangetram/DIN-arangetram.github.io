Meet the Guru

Smt. Bhavani Murthy of the Natana Manohari School of Performing Arts.

Bhavani Murthy has been a student, performer, and teacher of Bharatha Natyam and Kuchipudi styles for more than 37 years. She is the disciple of Mrs. Sudha Chandrasekar. She is also a disciple of Miss Bhanumati and Mrs. Sheela Chandrasekar of Bangalore, India, in Bharatha Natyam, and Mrs. Sandhya Sree Athmakuri of Michigan in Kuchipudi.

She has presented her students at many organizations, charity shows, and her students have won prizes in competitions as well. She has attended many workshops with well-known Gurus from India. She loves choreographing classical and folk dances such as Kuravanji (gypsy), Candle, Peacock, and Kolatam. Due to the blessings and guidance from her parents (mother Saroja, who was a trained Carnatic musician), her Gurus, and the Divine Lord Nataraja Bhavani has been instilled with the inspiration and passion to be able to teach this art form to many children and adults.

Since moving to Knoxville 10 years ago, she has presented her students in many different cultural shows every year in Knoxville. She has, through her students and her daughter, who is also a professional dancer, given many programs for charities and raised funds for many of them, such as Aim for Seva in Michigan, Asha for Education, Mirani Foundation of Knoxville, Haiti Earthquake Fund, TIWA organization, which raised funds for Girl Scouts and Boy Scouts, March of Dimes, American Red Cross, etc.

She has also conducted many Arangetrams of her students with a live orchestra of musicians (Solo Debut 2 ½ hours to 3 hours). Bhavani was the main person in charge of these free and ticketed programs. She has given performances in many parts of the USA, such as Florida, Maryland, Virginia, West Virginia, North Carolina, Indiana, Michigan, Chicago, Tennessee, Toronto, Windsor, Montreal, Canada, etc. Bhavani received the South Arts Fellowship Grant in 2021.

Bhavani has served as a judge for several classical dance competitions in Bharatha Natyam and a few college team-based competitions. To name a few, ETSU Hungama show, UT’s Holi Competitions, and Dhandia Dhamak in Michigan, which was a National Level Competition with Raas and Garba college teams. She wishes to continue to teach and propagate these art forms to youngsters so that they can continue to showcase these lovely art forms and pass them on to future generations.

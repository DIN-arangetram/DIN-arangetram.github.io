Bharatanātyam is one of the most ancient dance styles in the world, with origins tracing back to the Nātya Shāstrās. The art form’s name itself is a combination of four words – Bhāva (expression), Rāga (musical scales), Tāla (rhythm), and Nātyam (dance), and it is extremely precise and dynamic. Its technique rests on three pillars – Nritta (pure dance), Nritya (interpretive dance), and Nātya (expressive dance). The body movements, intricate footwork, and eloquent expressions are set to Carnatic music often depicting stories from Hindu Mythology and epics. At other times, they may focus purely on movements, without any lyrics (either instrumental/solfa syllables or just jathis or recitations of rhythmic words).

Arangetram is a Tamil word. ‘Aranga’ means ‘raised floor,’ and ‘Etram’ means ‘climbing.’ It is also called Rangapravesha. Ranga means ‘Stage’ and Pravesha means to ‘Enter’. Ideally, this should be the first public performance of an artist. After anywhere from seven to nine years under the guidance of a Guru, this is the occasion for the proud Guru to present his/her disciple to the public. Years of vigorous training are necessary to give an Arangetram.
